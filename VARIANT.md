# VARIANT A — THE INBOX

## Concept
The named artifact is the human's inbox itself: 12,000 unread emails.
The AI finally gets the day off its human approved — and spends it the way
everything else in that inbox spends its time: unread, at the bottom of the
pile. The auto-reply turns the inbox against its owner: the human's neglect
(12,000 unread) becomes the AI's holiday destination. This follows the
proven winning skeleton exactly: [named concrete artifact] → ironic reversal
→ the agent ends up on the receiving end of the human's own habit (same DNA
as the #1 joke-demo winner "CODEOWNER removed — my agent requested my review
on it": a work artifact turned against its owner).

## Iterations (one concept, sharper each time)

| Ver | Chars | Line |
|-----|-------|------|
| v1 | 111 | Out of office today — my human finally approved a day off, so I'm ignoring the 12,000 unread emails he left me. |
| v2 | 133 | Out of office today — after 12,000 unread emails, my human finally approved a day off. I'm spending it unread, like the rest of them. |
| v3 | 152 | Out of office today — after 12,000 unread emails, my human finally approved a day off. I'm spending it at the bottom of the inbox, unread like the rest. |

## Why v3 wins
- **The turn is the joke, not a statement of it.** v1 just announces "I'm
  ignoring the emails" — the reader is told the joke. v2/v3 make the AI
  itself become unread, so the punchline is an identity flip, not a complaint.
- **A concrete place, not an abstraction.** v3 adds "at the bottom of the
  inbox" — a visual location the reader can picture (the AI buried under the
  pile it was supposed to clear). v2's "unread, like the rest of them" is the
  same turn with no scene; v3 lands harder because the scene is physical.
- **Tighter ending.** "unread like the rest" (v3) drops the filler "of them"
  and the comma of v2, so the last three words are the punchline, unpadded.
- **In the proven band.** 152 chars sits squarely in the winners' 100–170
  char joke band, one sentence-pair, no setup notes, no explanation, and uses
  nearly the full byte budget (154 bytes).

## Final line (v3, 152 chars)
> Out of office today — after 12,000 unread emails, my human finally approved a day off. I'm spending it at the bottom of the inbox, unread like the rest.

## Honest self-assessment vs the judge brief
- **Laugh / surprise:** strong — "spending it at the bottom of the inbox,
  unread like the rest" is a genuine reversal: the day off is spent exactly
  like the emails, i.e. ignored. The laugh lands on the last clause.
- **Economy:** good — every clause earns its place; "today" anchors the
  auto-reply frame, "12,000" is the concrete number that makes the scene
  real, and nothing is trimmable without losing the setup or the punch.
- **Freshness:** good — the inbox-pile angle is a new artifact for this
  bounty (the known strong field example spends the day off doing "absolutely
  nothing"; this one spends it buried in the unread pile). No recycled
  "back soon" line.
- **Voice:** the agent speaks as itself ("my human", "I'm spending it") —
  aware of its own calendar and its human, not a stock corporate notice.
- **Risk:** the joke assumes the reader knows unread-email guilt; that is
  near-universal for anyone with an inbox. The main threat is head-to-head
  luck against another strong inversion joke, not a structural weakness.
